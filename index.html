Essay · 04

“Portrait of Mrs. P. in the South” by Paul Klee

Paul Klee’s Portrait of Mrs. P. in the South has fascinated me. This painting is a clear example of why art captivates me. In it, different emotions blend in such a way that the result is humor tinged with traces of love.

The first thing that caught my eye was the use of warm colors, which convey a special energy—almost as if the Mediterranean sun were streaming directly from the canvas. But what made me linger was Mrs. P.’s expression. There is something in her face that is both sophisticated and ridiculous. Her hat, which should protect her from the sun, barely covers her, and her face seems split between a subtle smile and a mouth left slightly open, stifled by the heat. Her eyes also carry that duality: they appear lost, frightened, confused by the weather or perhaps by life itself. That mix of emotions is what amuses me. It gives me the impression of a Palm Beach lady feeling vulnerable in unfamiliar territory.

And then there’s the heart on her chest. It’s easy to overlook, but even if it lingers in the subconscious, it has already reached us. At first glance, it might seem like just a quirk, but to me, it holds a much deeper meaning. In a world so marked by divisions, conflicts, and labels, we lose sight of what truly matters. That little heart becomes a silent reminder of the essential, of what connects us beyond everything that separates us.

For a long time, I wondered what art is. According to the Royal Spanish Academy, it is the activity of creating works that, through plastic, visual, auditory, or literary means, produce aesthetic or intellectual stimulation. But for me, that didn’t encompass everything. Art meant so much more. This thought was circling in my mind while traveling around Menorca, going from beach to beach with a backpack, touring the entire island with two friends. So, I decided to ask them, and they answered just like the Academy. Yet for me, it still didn’t quite fit.

I kept asking myself questions until one day, while walking through the endless fields of Burgos, I decided to call my uncle Fran, a truly genuine artist.

Without overthinking, I asked: “What is art? How would you define it?”

At first, I assumed he would give me the usual answer, but not only did he not do that, he also managed to silence all those voices in my head. “Art is love,” he responded.

And then I understood everything. He didn’t have to explain any further.

Through this painting, Klee shows how art can be a blend of seemingly contradictory emotions that, when combined, become something profoundly human. The piece speaks to me of vulnerability, humor, and a sensitivity that transcends what is visible.

Finally, the heart on the chest is a reminder of what truly matters in such a polarized world: the essential that connects us. After all, “art is love.” And perhaps that is what makes it so thrilling—its ability to reach the heart, beyond shapes and colors.
